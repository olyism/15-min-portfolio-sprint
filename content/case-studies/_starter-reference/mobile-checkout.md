## Problem

### Almost half of mobile checkouts were abandoned at the payment step.

Kitebank's checkout was designed for desktop, then squeezed onto phones. The primary button sat at the top of the screen, out of thumb reach. Card entry used six separate fields. Errors only appeared after you pressed "Pay".

## Solution

### I moved every important action into the thumb zone and removed half the fields.

- **Thumb-first layout.** The primary action sits at the bottom, with a sticky total that's always visible.
- **Smart card entry.** One field that detects the card type, plus Apple Pay and Google Pay as the default.
- **Inline validation.** Errors appear as you type, in plain language. "This card has expired" instead of "Error 402".

We tested three variations with 24 people in guerrilla sessions, then ran a two-week A/B test on 10% of traffic before rolling it out to everyone.

## Impact

### Mobile conversion rose 11%, and payment support tickets fell 27%.

- **Drove revenue:** checkout conversion on mobile up **11%**, worth about $2.1M a year.
- **Saved money:** payment-related support tickets down **27%**.
- **Recognition:** Good Design Award 2022, Digital category.

## Lessons

### The fastest way to convince people was to put the old checkout in their hands.

Stakeholder buy-in came from a 5-minute "try to pay with one hand" exercise in the exec meeting, not from the research deck.
