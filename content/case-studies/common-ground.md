## Problem

### A quote-comparison product assumes people are ready to ask for quotes.

Common Ground is a fictional service exploring home energy upgrades. The hypothetical brief proposes an instant comparison tool. That assumes the main obstacle is price, and that households already know which work they need, who can approve it and which providers they trust.

My research question would precede that assumption: **what prevents a household from taking its next informed step?** Renters, owners, apartment residents and people managing costs may face different barriers. A single confident persona would conceal those differences.

This is a research proposal with illustrative synthesis artifacts. No interviews, quotations or fieldwork are represented as real. The example notes below exist to demonstrate traceability and competing explanations, not to provide evidence about actual households.

## Solution

### I designed the study around a decision the team could genuinely change.

The decision is whether to build quote comparison first or a guided readiness service. Before recruitment, the team would agree what evidence could change that choice and what it cannot learn within seven weeks.

**Recruit for variation.** The proposed matrix crosses housing tenure, dwelling type, progress through an upgrade and access needs. Recruitment through a single provider would overrepresent people who have already acted, so the plan includes community channels and people who stopped before requesting a quote.

**Ask about a recent event.** Interviews would reconstruct the last attempt: what triggered it, which information was used, who else was involved and where the effort stopped. With consent, participants could show the material they relied on. We would not infer behaviour from hypothetical enthusiasm.

**Keep the evidence chain intact.** Each observation gets a source identifier, context and confidence note. Interpretation is stored separately from the observation. Counterexamples stay in the synthesis instead of being removed because they weaken a clean story.

**Turn findings into an explicit decision.** A workshop would compare the two product bets against observed barriers. The recommendation would include who benefits, who remains excluded, the evidence that supports it and what further study could reverse it.

The interactive ledger uses synthetic notes to illustrate this discipline. Selecting a theme changes the observation, interpretation and proposed next action together. The house photograph is AI-generated editorial imagery, not fieldwork documentation.

## Impact

### Research succeeds when it improves a decision, including a decision not to build.

There are no study findings or measured outcomes in this fictional case. The proposed impact record would capture the original product bet, the evidence considered, the eventual decision and its owner.

- **Decision quality:** can the team explain what changed and which evidence changed it?
- **Coverage:** which parts of the recruitment matrix remain unrepresented?
- **Traceability:** can another researcher find the source behind a recommendation?
- **Follow-through:** does the next prototype test the uncertainty the research actually exposed?

If the evidence supports the original comparison tool, that is a valid result. Research should not be rewarded only for producing a dramatic pivot.

## How I used AI

### Accelerate navigation through evidence without outsourcing interpretation.

With explicit consent and an approved data environment, AI could help index transcripts and retrieve candidate passages. Every passage would link back to the source and be checked for context. Identifying details should be removed before any permitted processing.

Synthetic people are useful for testing the discussion guide’s wording, but their answers are not participant evidence. This starter uses invented examples precisely so no one mistakes generated material for a completed research programme.

## Lessons

### A useful research artifact makes its limits as visible as its recommendation.

A tidy affinity map can hide missing groups and weak inferences. I would carry counterexamples, unanswered questions and recruitment gaps into the readout, alongside the recommendation.

For a real portfolio, include one decision before and after research, an anonymized evidence trail and a moment when a plausible interpretation did not survive scrutiny.
