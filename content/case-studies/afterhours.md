## Problem

### The cinema sells a night out, but the website behaves like a timetable.

Afterhours is a fictional neighbourhood cinema with a small team and an eclectic programme. The proposed commission joins two problems: the cinema needs a recognisable identity, and visitors need to find a suitable screening without deciphering a dense programme.

The client would have limited time for content entry and no appetite for replacing its ticketing provider. A beautiful bespoke booking system would create an unnecessary operational obligation. The brief therefore covers identity, programme discovery and the handoff to ticketing.

This example is an invented agency engagement. The cinema, films, commercial constraints and proposed outcomes are illustrative. The programme artwork and film stills are AI-generated, not photographs or released films from a real client.

## Solution

### I used the character of a printed film programme, with the clarity of a good booking flow.

The identity pairs an expressive editorial serif with strong sans-serif utility text, ink-black and oxblood surfaces, a vermilion accent and warm screen-like type. It is intentionally different from the surrounding portfolio. Film titles get room to breathe; dates and screening times remain practical.

**Shape the commission before designing.** The proposed scope has three acceptance milestones: identity direction, a responsive programme prototype and a ticketing handoff. Each milestone names the client inputs, review window and included revisions. New integrations would be estimated separately.

**Make discovery finite.** The programme starts with a day and a small selection of screenings. Duration, classification and access information belong with the film. Showtimes are actions, not decorative type. The prototype demonstrates a local selection without selling a real ticket.

**Protect operations.** The production proposal would adapt the existing ticket provider’s feed rather than ask staff to maintain two programmes. Price, availability and final confirmation would remain authoritative in the provider’s system. The site must handle an unavailable feed with clear next steps.

**Build a durable handover.** The final deliverables would include editable templates, content guidance and a short operating guide. An agency project is not finished when the launch looks good; the client must be able to publish next month’s programme without another redesign.

The local seat-selection exercise below is a concept demonstration only. It never reserves a seat, takes payment or contacts a booking service.

## Impact

### Commercial success includes a client who can operate the work independently.

No bookings, revenue or client outcomes have been measured. The proposed evaluation combines a programme-finding task with a rehearsal of the staff publishing workflow.

- **Visitor outcome:** find an appropriate film and reach the correct screening’s ticketing page.
- **Booking handoff:** maintain selected film and showtime through the provider transition.
- **Client outcome:** publish a new programme and correct an error without developer assistance.
- **Delivery:** meet agreed scope and acceptance criteria, with changes recorded rather than absorbed invisibly.

A real post-launch review should distinguish improvements caused by the site from changes in programme popularity, pricing or marketing spend.

## How I used AI

### Use AI to explore atmosphere while keeping the identity and service coherent.

The cinema programme image and its film stills were generated for the concept. It establishes atmosphere without implying access to a real venue. The prompt and asset provenance are documented with the project.

For a client, generated campaign imagery would require agreement about usage, representation and any real film assets. Film stills, posters and credits would come from the distributor’s approved material, not invented replacements.

## Lessons

### A strong commission resolves the business constraint as carefully as the visual brief.

The expressive identity earns attention, but the project’s value depends on a usable programme and a maintainable ticketing handoff. Scoping those boundaries is part of the design work.

For your own agency case study, include the original brief, a scope decision, the client collaboration and what the client could do independently after handover.
