# Portfolio case study best practices

Reference when drafting or critiquing portfolio case studies. Keeps SKILL.md lean; load when refining narrative or checking against industry norms.

## Structure norms

- **Overview**: Project name, your role, duration, and a one-line summary. In the template this is frontmatter (`title`, `catchphrase`, `role`, `timeline`, `team`, `tools`).
- **Section shape**: Within each section (Problem, Solution, Impact, How I used AI, Lessons): **## headline → ### compelling TL;DR one-liner → body**. The one-liner sums up the section and value prop so the audience can scan and understand quickly, then read in detail as needed.
- **Problem**: Clear problem statement, goals, constraints, and user/business pain. Avoid vague “we needed to improve things.”
- **Process/approach**: Your approach, key decisions, and how you overcame obstacles—not a generic “we did research, then designed.” Name tactics (e.g. Wizard of Oz MVP, progressive disclosure, anticipatory design).
- **Outcomes & impact**: Results with numbers or clear evidence; link back to decisions. Separate **Impact** (what happened) from **Lessons** (what you’d do again or do differently).

## What top portfolios do

- **Story over process**: The narrative drives. Readers remember “we validated with a Google Form and learned X” more than “we followed the double diamond.”
- **Insights over steps**: Highlight what you learned, what failed, and why you pivoted. Show judgment, not just output.
- **You over standards**: Explain your reasoning and trade-offs. Don’t default to textbook process; show how you adapted to context (e.g. tight ratios, legacy systems).
- **Outcome-focused and reflective**: Tie choices to results. End with concise, honest takeaways that demonstrate continuous learning.

## Audience-specific emphasis

| Audience | Emphasize |
|----------|-----------|
| Consulting/coaching clients | Speed to value, working with constraints, validating before building, clear ownership of strategy and execution |
| Hiring managers (top companies) | Scope (strategy → execution), collaboration with eng/product, bar for quality, scale or complexity handled |

## Showing AI fluency without hype

- Name the tool *and* the job it did: "Claude Code scaffolded five prototype variations in four days" beats "used AI to speed things up".
- Show judgment: what you checked by hand, what you didn't delegate, and how you knew the output was right (evals, spot checks, user tests).
- Tie it to the outcome: AI is interesting to readers only when it changed speed, scope, cost or quality.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Long tool/method tutorials (Figma, “design thinking”) unless central to the story.
- Vague outcomes (“improved user satisfaction”) without evidence or metrics.
- Hiding your role in “we” without showing your specific contribution.
- Invented or inflated metrics. Use ranges or proxies and label estimates.
- Overlong case studies; prefer tight narrative and optional “deep dives” or appendices if needed.
